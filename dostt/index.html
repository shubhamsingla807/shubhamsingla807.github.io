Q1 · User Activation

40% install and never start a conversation

The real problem

A new user lands with 0 coins and the first thing pushed is a coin purchase. They are asked to pay before talking to anyone, and before they even know what coins are for. The cartoon avatars make a "talk to someone real" pitch feel fake. The drop-off sits at a wall that costs money and looks uncertain, not a failure to understand the value.

Assumption: installs come from wellness or friendship ads, users arrive curious but low-intent, and expect to try before paying.

H1

The upfront coin wall blocks the first call

TestGive some % of new installs 15 free coins on signup, keep the rest as is, compare first-conversation rate in 48h.
RiskActivation gets bought with free minutes, hurting margins and pulling in freeloaders who never top up.
H2

AI cartoon avatars break trust

TestA/B the profile card with trust signals (online now, "320 calls taken", short real voice clip) vs the cartoon card. Measure tap-to-call.
RiskThe credibility problem is catalog-wide, and fixing it may clash with anonymity or expose weak profiles.
H3

A cold voice call is too big a first step

TestGive a cohort free text-chat with a real listener before calling, compare activation against call-first.
RiskA text tier monetises worse than calls and could cannibalise paid minutes.
H4

We never capture why they came

TestAdd a one-tap intent question in onboarding (lonely, vent, curious, advice), reorder home to match, compare first-conversation rate.
RiskMatching is only as good as supply. Thin supply for a chosen intent makes the empty state worse.

How I'd read it: one metric across all four, the percent of new installs who finish a first real conversation within 48 hours. Whichever lever moves it most is the real leak.

Q2 · Feature Success Metrics

Leading indicators for Random Calling

The real problem

Random Calling exists to remove the "who do I even call" decision. One tap and you are connected. So the leading signals should measure whether that shortcut gets people into good conversations faster, well before any top-up. I leave out "% who pick random", because profiles own the screen and pull the clicks, so it always reads low and looks like a false negative.

Assumption: listener quality is roughly even on both paths, so we measure the feature and not a lucky or unlucky voice.

1

Time to first connect

ReadRandom-available cohort vs not. If random shrinks the median time from opening the app to being in a live call, it is doing its core job.
2

Connection quality

ReadShare of random taps that reach a live listener, together with the share of those calls lasting past 2-3 minutes. Speed plus staying power.
3

Time to value

ReadRandom-first vs profile-first users, how quickly each starts a second call. Faster second call means value delivered early.

Held for later: share of calls that are random vs specific. It reads low no matter what, so I would only grow it on purpose once Indicator 3 proves random users come back faster.

Q3 · Scalability

Concurrent calls double overnight

The real problem

A caller-side demand spike from a festival or the 80% off offer. Listener supply cannot double by morning, and every failure below flows from that gap.

Assumption: listeners online stay roughly flat while caller demand doubles.

Supply runs dry

FailCallers tap and no one is free. Failed connects, dead home screen. Liquidity is the whole product, so this hurts most.

The economy leaks

FailAn 80% off sale sells minutes cheap while listeners still earn full rupees a minute. Bigger losses, plus bargain hunters who never top up.

Broken first run for new users

FailFestival installs hit the Q1 wall, amplified. A bigger inflow also brings bad actors pushing NSFW calls, souring it for listeners who then quit.

Safety load spikes

FailDouble the calls, double the reports, but moderation is human. Female listeners get flooded and some go offline, shrinking supply further.
↓ NPS / ratings

A surge of 1-star Play Store reviews or a rising uninstall rate.

↓ Second-call initiation

Ties straight to fewer wallet top-ups.

↓ Minutes per call

Volume doubled, but that says nothing about quality.

Q3 · Prioritize & Address

Fix what loses money or users for good, first

1

Protect the connection

Surge payouts to pull listeners online, notify offline ones, pre-schedule supply if the festival is known ahead. If demand still overshoots, throttle with a queue and a wait estimate so calls stop failing silently.

2

Protect the money

Cap discounted coins per user, block stacking the offer with spin-wheel freebies, and watch a live payout-to-revenue ratio.

3

Protect the new-user wave

Give the festival cohort one genuinely free first call and route scarce listeners to first-timers, so the spike does not burn the users the ads just paid for.

4

Protect trust

Tighter rate limits, faster auto-flagging, and a clear report path so listeners feel safe enough to stay online.

Q4 · Retention Loop

3 calls done, silent for 7 days

The real problem

Waiting for 7 days of silence is already too late. By then some users have uninstalled, and once the app is gone no nudge reaches them and re-installs almost never happen. The real work is catching the churn while they are still installed and warm, so I replace the fixed trigger with an early-warning signal from behaviour.

Assumption: these users came for an emotional reason, so the pull back should be human, and the drop-off is either about money or about not feeling the value yet.

  • Predict the churn before it happens. Build a risk signal from patterns, not a calendar: falling call frequency, shorter calls, calls dropped mid-way, opening the app without calling, spin-only sessions, coins at zero with no top-up. Act while they are still on the app.
  • Fix the real reason they stopped. Money or a value that never clicked. One move covers both, a free 2-minute call with the best-matched listener. Removes the cost barrier and buys another shot at the value.
  • Guide the next connection. Don't drop an at-risk user onto a generic home. Point them to who to talk to next based on history and interests.
  • Nudge journey built on psychology. One adaptive journey, not timed copies. Lead hook is a personal follow-up from their last listener referencing the real chat they had. If ignored, shift levers from the familiar person, to the unmet need, to a low-friction free minute.
  • Chat as the soft re-entry. A cooling-off user won't jump into a paid voice call. Let the follow-up land as a free chat they can reply to, warm up, then move to a call.

Measure: does the churn model catch at-risk users before they go dormant, does the intervention lift return rate vs a holdout, and how many free 2-minute calls convert into a 4th paid call.
